# How the pipeline became a graph

## What ran in August

An agent ran the shoots. I wrote a dated doctrine file, about four hundred lines of rules and the rulings behind them, and a Claude Code agent read it and drove the scripts in this repository from it, the boards, prompts, engine calls, the voice, the closer, the build, the gates and delivery. The gates that could block a call were Claude Code hooks, `guards/prop_gate.sh`, `guards/block_unpinned_identity.sh` and `guards/ship_gate.sh`. The agent wrote the ledgers under `shoots/` by hand. I reviewed every delivered cut and sent back the ones that were wrong, which the ledgers record as withdrawals.

## What the compile changed

Once the runs converged on one path, that path became a LangGraph, [`pipeline/graph.py`](../pipeline/graph.py).

The step order and the gates are edges and plain functions now, so the order is kept by code instead of by an agent remembering it, and delivery is unreachable without a ship-gate pass. The judgements no metric makes are two interrupts. The eye rules before anything ships, on a mouth REVIEW, a lag worth a nudge, a prop the edge probe saw cut by the frame, a scene that runs the wrong way, or a replay. An answer the graph cannot act on is asked again, not guessed at. The review comes after delivery, because that is where the ledgers show I looked. Every loop has a ceiling. The ledger is written by code, [`pipeline/ledger.py`](../pipeline/ledger.py), in one schema, [`pipeline/SCHEMA.md`](../pipeline/SCHEMA.md), with real request ids, times to the millisecond, and identity ids replaced by a hash. A live run checkpoints every step to SQLite, so it can wait days for a person and resume where it stopped, or be re-entered just before a step that failed on a defect since fixed, without paying again for the steps before it.

## How I know it matches

[`pipeline/replay.py`](../pipeline/replay.py) reads the seven August ledgers against the graph and counts only the moves a row states. Every one has an edge. Where history and the graph disagree, the replay prints it. Three REVIEWs shipped with no eye verdict on the record, and eighteen deliveries in the ads7 batch have no ship-gate pass. Four loops ran past a bound the graph now enforces, one scene re-rolled twice with no reason recorded and three spots each sent back twice for what a scene showed, four of those six send-backs naming no one. It also lists what the August rows never recorded, board verdicts, voice takes, build parameters, real request ids, which is what the graph writes on every run. `make replay` prints the whole report, and `tests/test_replay.py` pins it.

## The first spot through the graph

Z.ai, from the ads8 board, shot live with three Omni scenes, a fresh narration drawn three times and matched to its script, and the closer take reused from August, since the HeyGen REST API bills a wallet of its own, apart from the web plan's credits. It stopped five times on defects since fixed, and each stop is on its [ledger](../shoots/graph-zai/ledger.jsonl). The crop assumed 1080p scenes and Omni now returns 720p, two path bugs broke the build, the gates were handed captions away from the segments they measure, and the replay probe was pointed at the ten-second closer instead of the master. After each fix the run was re-entered from the checkpoint before the failing step, and nothing was paid for twice. The gates then passed, captions on five cues, the closer placed at 0 ms, the mouth at correlation 0.43, loudness at -16.1 LUFS.

A review of the code then found two things the run had got past. The ship gate had waived a replay it found at 20 seconds with nobody reading it, and the render had never shown anyone the two scenes its edge probe flagged. Both now go to the eye, and the run was re-entered for them at no cost, since every scene, the narration and the closer were already held. It is waiting at the eye for a person to look at those two scenes. After that the ship gate runs on the rebuilt master, and a replay it finds there goes to the eye as well.

## One girl through the whole story

When I watched that cut, the girl in the story changed from one scene to the next, and the August run of the same spot had done the same. Every scene named "a student", and a text prompt cannot hold a face, so the engine drew its own student each time.

The first fix held every scene to the presenter, the narrator who closes the spot. That made the scenes consistent, and it put the narrator into the story, which is not the point. The girl in the story is someone else, and she has to stay the same girl. That run, [`shoots/graph-zai-cast`](../shoots/graph-zai-cast/ledger.jsonl), is kept as history, waiting at the eye with three scenes that read 0.53, 0.63 and 0.65 against the narrator. It also ran into a fal account that went out of credit mid-run, where a finished render was held back and then dropped by the vendor, and a re-entry that tried to send a scene it already held and was stopped only by the lock. A re-entry now asks the ledger whether a take is held, and a job the vendor has dropped is confirmed gone twice before its scene is sent again.

The rule now is one character per story, held to her own face. A board writes `{character}` where she appears, and [`gates/board_probe.py`](../gates/board_probe.py) sends back a scene that names anyone else, the narrator included. Her face comes from a take of her, for this spot the girl in the August Z.ai scene a, and every scene that shows her is rendered on Omni's reference path from it, at the same price per scene. Before anything is paid for, her face is read against the narrator's and has to fall under the floor, and it reads 0.12. Then [`gates/cast_gate.py`](../gates/cast_gate.py) reads every scene back against both. It refuses a scene under 0.30 against her, one with a second person in it who is not her, and one that reads at least as close to the narrator as to her. The floor alone cannot do that last part. The narrator rendered into one of these scenes read 0.40 against the girl, over the floor, and 0.53 against the narrator. Nor can it tell apart two girls the engine draws from one prompt, which read 0.50 to 0.66 against her, and that is why her scenes are rendered from her face rather than left to a prompt. A prompt rewritten at the eye meets the board's check before anything is rendered from it.

The narration had slipped the same way. My doctrine says the voice talks to the viewer, never about the character, and its example of what not to do is this spot's own line, "Her studio apartment is smaller than the problem she's solving." The board still said it. The next shoot ran from [`shoots/graph-zai-character/boards.json`](../shoots/graph-zai-character/boards.json), which says "Your studio apartment is smaller than the problem you're solving," and a seventh board check sends back any narration with he, she, his, her, him or hers in it.

That run, [`shoots/graph-zai-character`](../shoots/graph-zai-character/ledger.jsonl), kept her the same girl, 0.90 to 0.93 against her face and 0.08 to 0.11 against the narrator's, and it was boring. The engine copied the face reference's close-up into every scene, so every shot was her face in dim light, and the room the board describes never expanded. The girl carried through and the story did not.

So a spot can now shoot its scenes as a chain, and each scene starts from a frame instead of a face. The first starts from a whole still of her, the first frame of the August take, and every later scene starts from the last frame of the scene before it, on Omni's image-to-video path at the same price. She and the room carry through, and the prompt moves them. A scene is sent only after the one before it landed and was read back, and a scene that fails stops the chain there, so nothing is paid for on top of a take that is about to be thrown away. A take is kept only when it started from the frame its scene would start from now, so a new take early in the chain shoots every scene after it again. An eighth board check holds the chain to the spot's own scenes, each once. That shoot ran from [`shoots/graph-zai-chain/boards.json`](../shoots/graph-zai-chain/boards.json).

That run, [`shoots/graph-zai-chain`](../shoots/graph-zai-chain/ledger.jsonl), held her, 0.78 to 0.82 against her face and 0.11 to 0.15 against the narrator's, and I still found it almost boring. Measured against the August cut, the script and the prompts were the same word for word. The chain had let the camera creep in, her face growing from a quarter of the frame's height to a third, so the mug that is the ad's one quirk and the size of the warehouse never showed. And the build had one scene to each sentence, so the long middle sentence outran its five second scene, which was slowed to 1.6 times and held on its last frame, about two seconds where nothing moves. The August cut had the same freeze. So a sentence can now hold more than one shot, joined in order before the build cuts it, and every shot names its camera. A ninth board check holds those slots to the three sentences the build cuts, every scene once, and the cast gate reads a face too small to recognise, her figure far off in a wide shot, as no face, which goes to the eye. The next shoot runs from [`shoots/graph-zai-shots/boards.json`](../shoots/graph-zai-shots/boards.json), four shots under three sentences. It opens on the walls already shooting away around her, rises to a high wide shot where she is small in the warehouse with the mug in frame, comes down as the warehouse folds back into the same studio, and ends on her typing beside the mug. A board can also say where the switching sound plays, under the sentence breaks as before, nowhere, or where the picture changes most, since in a chained cut the picture does not change where the sentences break, and a tenth board check refuses any mode the build does not know.

## What is mine and what the agent did

I wrote the doctrine, chose every gate and probe and the threshold each one enforces, and graded the exemplars those thresholds are derived from. The agent ran the August shoots from that doctrine, drafted the boards and prompts, and kept the ledgers. The code here was written with Claude Code, under my direction and my review, and each change went through an adversarial review before it landed.

## What stays out

Look generation stays out, because it drives a web endpoint through a signed-in browser. The doctrine stays out, because it names identities and private paths, and so do the private delivery channel and the identity pins. Every file the code calls that is not in the repository is listed with its reason in [`tests/test_references.py`](../tests/test_references.py), and the test fails if a new one appears.
